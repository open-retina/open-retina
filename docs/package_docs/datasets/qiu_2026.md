# Qiu et al., 2026

This dataset contains responses of mouse retinal ganglion cell **axonal boutons**, imaged with chronic
two-photon calcium imaging in the superficial layers of the superior colliculus of awake mice watching
natural movie clips. Unlike the other datasets in OpenRetina, the recordings are made at the retinal
*output* rather than in the retina itself, and every session ships the animal's behaviour (pupil size,
locomotion) and eye position alongside the responses.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| mouse (awake) | superior colliculus, superficial layers | 2P imaging of RGC axonal boutons | natural movie clips, 10 s | 1 (grayscale) | 2 of 3 used (pupil size, locomotion) | 17,272 of 63,747 recorded | 10 (3 animals) | 30 Hz | 30 Hz | 36 × 64 px |

Bouton counts are the lengths of the shipped `neurons_fluor_good` masks, counted from the data rather than
taken from the paper.

rather than drawing validation clips at random.

## Two test designs

Sessions do not share one test paradigm, which matters when pooling results across them:

- **Six sessions** (`18-4`, `19-9`, `3-8`, `4-4`, `5-8`, `6-5`) were tested on **the same six clips**, each
  repeated 14–20 times. These are the sessions with plenty of training data (320–420 train clips).
- **Four sessions** (`16-3`, `16-5`, `17-2`, `2-7`) were instead tested on **94–96 distinct clips** with only
  1–10 repeats each, and have roughly a quarter of the training data (~100 train clips). Each of these sets
  is a superset of the six shared clips.

Across all ten sessions there are 96 distinct test clips, of which **6 are common to every session** — the
only ones for which a cross-session, repeat-averaged comparison is well powered.

## Other stimuli in the file tree

Each session's `test` tier also holds trials that are not natural-movie clips, and these differ by session:
functional-characterisation movies (`chirp`, `moving bar`) in five sessions, and `monet2` / `trippy`
parametric noise in the four small-training sessions. The OpenRetina loaders select natural-movie trials via
`stimulus_type="clip"` and ignore the rest, which also protects the repeat-stacking code from the
frame-count jitter those trials carry.

## Dataset structure

The data ships in Sensorium FileTree format — one `.npy` per trial per stream, with trial `i` of every
stream describing the same trial, aligned to row `i` of each `meta/trials/*.npy` array:

```text
dynamic28188-19-9-Fluorescence-7b721b-v4a
├── data
│   ├── videos         # {i}.npy — (36, 64, T) grayscale movie
│   ├── responses      # {i}.npy — (n_boutons, T) raw calcium fluorescence
│   ├── behavior       # {i}.npy — (3, T) pupil size, Δ-pupil, locomotion
│   └── pupil_center   # {i}.npy — (2, T) eye position, input to the shifter
└── meta
    ├── trials         # tiers, condition_hash, clip_movie_name, … (one row per trial)
    ├── neurons        # unit_ids, cell_motor_coordinates, …
    └── statistics     # per-stream mean/std used for normalisation
```

Trials are NaN-padded at the end to a length that is uniform within a session but differs between sessions
(300, 450 or 960 frames, the longest trial that session contains); the valid portion of a clip trial is
always the first 300 frames. Neuron quality masks live one level up, in
`data-quality/<session-prefix>_neurons_fluor_good.npy`, and are **index** arrays rather than boolean masks.
